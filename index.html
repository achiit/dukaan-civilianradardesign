Civilian Radar System

High-Level Conceptual Design Document

Project: 1000 m Civilian Object Detection and Tracking Radar Document type: Technical architecture reference Design level: Conceptual Purpose: Non-personal situational awareness

This document outlines a civilian-grade radar concept for detecting and tracking objects at distances of up to 1000 metres. The system outputs kinematic information only; it does not capture imagery, identify individuals, or create personal profiles.

2.2 Track Measurements

  • Range: Estimated distance from the radar, in metres.
  • Azimuth: Horizontal direction relative to the sensor.
  • Velocity: Estimated radial movement speed, in metres per second.
  • Class: Broad category only where supported by validated measurements; otherwise UNKNOWN.
  • Status: New, active, temporarily lost, or terminated.
  • Confidence: Optional quality indicator for measurement and track reliability.

2.3 Unique Track Identification

Each track receives a UUID and a readable, session-scoped label such as TARGET-001. The tracking software associates subsequent measurements with existing tracks, preserves labels while continuity remains reliable, and retires labels when tracks terminate. A track identifier is a software reference—not proof of a physical object's identity.

3.2 Layer Responsibilities

  • Hardware Abstraction Layer: Interfaces with approved hardware, reports status and faults, and exposes safety-state information.
  • DSP Processing Layer: Processes digitized measurements, estimates range and radial velocity, filters clutter, and reports confidence.
  • Application Layer: Manages track lifecycles, associates measurements, assigns labels, stores limited track history, and generates zone alerts.
  • API Layer: Provides validated track data through authenticated, encrypted interfaces with role-based access, audit logging, and rate limiting.
  • Presentation Layer: Displays current tracks, system health, and alerts in an accessible interface.

4.2 Display Elements and Accessibility

  • Circular range rings, radar-centre marker, target markers, and readable labels.
  • Velocity direction indicators and a track-details panel.
  • Configurable detection zones and clear alert states.
  • System health, sensor status, and safe/ inhibited transmit state.
  • High-contrast colours, clear typography, adjustable scaling, and alerts that do not rely on colour alone.
  • Desktop and tablet support with simple navigation.

The interface must clearly state that displayed labels refer to radar tracks, not confirmed identities.

5. Compliance & Safety Notes

5.1 Radio-Frequency Compliance

  • Confirm the permitted operating band and licensing requirements with the relevant national spectrum authority.
  • For deployment in Pakistan, consult the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) and applicable spectrum regulations.
  • Obtain required equipment approvals and operating permissions before transmission.
  • Remain within authorized emission limits and assess interference with nearby radio services.
  • Do not operate outside authorized frequency, bandwidth, or power limits.

5.2 RF Exposure and Electrical Safety

  • Complete an RF exposure assessment using applicable ICNIRP guidance and local requirements.
  • Assess installation distances, grounding, electrical protection, and environmental conditions.
  • Provide hardware interlocks, transmit-inhibit functionality, and automatic shutdown for unsafe faults.
  • Document inspection, maintenance, and safe operating procedures.

5.3 Privacy Protection

  • Collect and display only the kinematic data required for situational awareness.
  • Do not implement facial recognition, biometrics, personal identity inference, audio/video capture, or personal tracking profiles.
  • Apply data minimization, retention limits, access controls, and applicable privacy laws, including GDPR where relevant.

5.4 Cybersecurity

  • Use TLS-protected communications and strong authentication.
  • Enforce role-based access control and audit security-relevant actions.
  • Protect configuration data, validate inputs, and rate-limit API access.
  • Use authenticated software updates and restrict configuration changes to authorized roles.

5.5 Operational Safety

  • Provide an emergency transmit-inhibit control and hardware-enforced power limits.
  • Use controlled startup and shutdown sequences and continuous fault monitoring.
  • Define safe-state behaviour for loss of control, overheating, or hardware failure.
  • Conduct periodic safety inspections and maintain operating records.
